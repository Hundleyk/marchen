# 04 · World and narrative

## The islands

A chain of islands in a warm, bright sea, with a deep drop-off to the west where the open ocean begins. Humans live on the larger islands; merfolk live in coves, on small islets and below the water. The two peoples have shared these waters for generations, mostly kindly, if not always closely.

| Place | Type | What it's like |
|---|---|---|
| **Brinehaven** | Main island, human town | A white-stone town stacked up a sunny cliffside, all stairs, terraces and narrow lanes winding down to a little harbour. A lighthouse above, the beach below. Warm, unhurried and friendly. Her new home. |
| **The Shelf** | Merfolk cove beside Brinehaven | The small merfolk community: houses on stilts and in the rocks, half in the water. The divers' shelter, the **Warming Ring**, sits on the shore. |
| **Saltpan Isle** | Human village | Salt-makers with their own dialect and old songs; windmills and dazzling white salt pans. |
| **Gull Rock** | Small island | The outer lighthouse and its keeper; bird cliffs, wind, and the best view of the sea. |
| **Ferrow's Reach** | Human fishing village | A quiet village of boat sheds and painted doors on the far side of the islands. Where Aunt Maren lives now. |
| **Kelpcrown Atoll** | Merfolk islet | An old merfolk settlement where the elders live and keep the old ways. |
| **The Pearl Banks** | Underwater | The Shelf divers' shared harvest grounds: oysters, abalone, urchins and sea-greens. |
| **The Drowned Quarter** | Underwater | An old part of Brinehaven that slipped into the sea in a storm long ago. A beautiful, quiet ruin full of fish and history. |
| **The Drop-off** | Deep water | The edge of the open ocean. The way home lies beyond it. |

## Tone

* **The town is kind.** Brinehaven's people are curious, a little clumsy around merfolk, and warm. Nobody is cruel to her.
* **No villains.** The story's challenges come from being new, from the sea and weather, from change, and from misunderstandings between people who care about each other.
* **Warmth through everyday life:** neighbours, friends, routines, small kindnesses, and two cultures slowly mixing.
* **A gentle ache underneath:** homesickness, a home that's changing, and the question of where she belongs.

## The story in five acts

### Prologue
Far beyond the islands lies **Coral Hollow**, Nix's reef village. The water there is slowly warming and the reef is fading. No one is to blame; it's just changing. Families have started to talk about whether they'll need to move one day. Nix's aunt **Maren** left for the islands years ago, and two years ago her letters stopped coming. Nix goes to Brinehaven to start a new life, see if it could be a home for her family too, and find out what happened to Maren.

### Act 1 · Arrival (spring)
Nix surfaces at the end of Brinehaven's pier at dawn. The flustered Harbourmaster doesn't quite know the procedure for merfolk, but he does his best and walks her up the stairs himself. The town is curious: kids follow her, the baker gives her a sweet bun "for the walk," and someone leaves a water bucket by the door of her new room on the first day. **Ada**, the lighthouse keeper's granddaughter, becomes her guide and first friend.

At Maren's old address there's only a note: *moved on, no forwarding*. At the Shelf, **Grandmother Wenna**, the eldest diver, takes Nix in and starts teaching her the divers' ways. That night Nix lights a single shell on the water at the end of the pier, a tradition from home called the Lighting of the Water. Ada waves from the lighthouse path.

**Ends with:** her first barnacle in Brinehaven, and the small mystery of where Maren went.

### Act 2 · Settling (summer)
Everyday life takes shape: dawn dives with the Shelf's divers, selling the harvest at the town market, afternoons exploring, evenings at the Warming Ring. She learns dockside words and teaches Ada merfolk ones. **Pim and Pell**, the merfolk twins, start at the town school; their classmates bombard them with questions, and Nix helps them find the words to answer. She gets customs wrong in funny ways (greeting the mayor forehead to forehead, gifting wet bread) and the town gets her customs wrong too (someone gifts her a fish dinner).

She explores Saltpan Isle with **Old Lis** and visits **Keeper Holm** at Gull Rock. Clues about Maren turn up: a carved comb, a woman's whistle heard across the water at dusk.

**Ends with:** the summer Lighting of the Water, with the Shelf joining her and townsfolk coming down to the pier to watch.

### Act 3 · Two worlds (autumn)
Nix is asked to carry messages to **Kelpcrown Atoll**, where the elders hold an old belief: *a life settled on land is a life given up by the sea.* They look kindly but warily at her new barnacles.

She finds Maren at last, in Ferrow's Reach, **married to a human fisherman, with a little daughter, Isla,** who's half-merfolk. Maren stopped writing home because she was afraid of what the elders and the family would think. She's happy, but she's lost touch with the sea and with her own people.

Nix becomes the bridge: carrying letters between Maren and Coral Hollow, bringing Isla to the Shelf to learn to swim and whistle, and gently arguing with the elders about what belonging really means.

**Ends with:** a letter from home: the reef is fading faster, and some families may come to the islands next spring.

### Act 4 · The storm (winter)
The worst winter storm in memory hits the islands. Fishing boats are caught out at sea; the Shelf's stilt houses are battered; part of the lower town floods.

Everyone pulls together. Nix and the merfolk divers guide boats home through the dark water while Keeper Holm keeps the light burning. Humans open their homes to merfolk whose houses are flooded. Maren's husband and the Shelf divers search the reefs side by side. Even the Kelpcrown elders come to help.

After the storm comes the quiet: rebuilding together, the two peoples closer than they've ever been. On the calmest winter nights Nix dives alone to the drop-off, where **marine snow** drifts down through the dark water, and thinks about home.

**Ends with:** the elders seeing humans and merfolk rebuild side by side.

### Act 5 · Belonging (spring again)
The town and the Shelf finish rebuilding, with merfolk shell work and human stonework mixed together. At the **Spring Current rite**, the Kelpcrown elders welcome Nix, Maren and little Isla as divers of the community, barnacles and all. Grandmother Wenna gives Nix her old diving stone.

The first families from Coral Hollow arrive at the pier. This time Nix is the one who knows the words, the customs and where to find water on dry days.

**Ending:** the Lighting of the Water, with the whole town on the pier: human children saying *"ulla,"* shell lanterns on every porch, Ada beside her. Home isn't one place anymore; it's both.

### Epilogue
A young merfolk girl surfaces at the end of the pier at dawn, clutching a letter of introduction. Nix goes down the steps to meet her.

## Story threads

### Merfolk children at the town school
Pim and Pell are the first merfolk pupils. Their classmates are fascinated and full of questions; the twins need water breaks and don't know all the words. Nix walks them to school and helps. By Act 5 the class uses merfolk words, and the teacher keeps a water basin by the door.

### The waterline belief
The Kelpcrown elders believe *a life settled on land is a life given up by the sea.* It's why they're wary of Nix's new barnacles, and why Maren stopped writing home. The story never treats the elders as wrong for caring about their culture; it shows them coming to see that you can belong to two places.

### Unspoken feelings
* **Ada** is drawn to Nix and to the sea, and never quite says so.
* **Rafe** has a hopeless crush on Corra.
* **Corra** acts prickly but is afraid of the old ways disappearing.
* **Keeper Holm** has never talked about his son.

### Ordinary life underwater
The Shelf's underwater half is as lived-in as the town: laundry swaying in the current, kids late for lessons, neighbours gossiping between doorways.
