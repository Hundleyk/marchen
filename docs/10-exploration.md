# 10 · Exploration

Exploration is how the player falls in love with the islands. It should feel like being new somewhere: every street corner and reef ledge might hold a person, a story or a view you didn't expect.

**Principles**
1. **Curiosity, not checklists.** No map icons for every collectible. Discoveries are found by looking, listening and following people.
2. **Everything found means something.** Objects belong to someone, places have history, creatures have merfolk names.
3. **Two halves, one map.** Most places have an above and a below, and the best secrets connect them.
4. **The world opens as she belongs.** New areas come from friendships, words, skills and seasons, not just keys.

---

## 1. Getting around

### On land
| Move | Notes |
|---|---|
| Walk / jog | Careful and a little unsteady in Act 1; confident by Act 3 |
| Climb | Low walls, ladders, rope nets, rocks; hand-over-hand on wet rocks (she's better on wet than dry) |
| Balance | Narrow beams, pier rails, mooring ropes |
| Jump | Between rocks and boats; she can leap into the water from any height |
| Rooftops | The harbour's net-lofts and rooftops connect; a route Pim and Pell show her |

### In water
| Move | Notes |
|---|---|
| Surface swim | Fast, relaxed; can talk to people on boats and piers |
| Dive | Full 3D swimming; a burst of speed with a flick |
| Glide | Hold still to drift and sink gently, good for looking around |
| Ride currents | Visible streams of drifting particles; carry her fast between places |
| Breach | Leap out of the water at speed onto piers, rocks and low decks |
| Squeeze | Slip through narrow gaps in reefs and wrecks |
| Rest | Hold onto a rock or kelp to slow Breath drain while listening or watching |

### Between islands
* **Swimming:** always possible, but the open water between some islands is long and has strong currents.
* **Currents:** once learned, fast routes between islands (see the *current map* below).
* **Ferry and fishing boats:** catch a ride (some captains won't take merfolk at first).
* **Her own boat:** a small sail-and-oar skiff she earns in Act 3; lets her carry human friends to other islands and explore at night.
* **Low-tide walks:** sandbars appear at low tide between Brinehaven and Saltpan Isle.

## 2. What changes the world

The same place is different depending on:

| Factor | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Tide** | Low: sandbars, tidepools, sea caves, wreck decks exposed. High: harbour streets flooded, boats out, deeper water reaches new ledges |
| **Time of day** | Dawn: divers and fishers out. Day: busy town. Dusk: lamplighters, the Warming Ring. Night: bioluminescence below; quiet streets; secret meetings |
| **Season** | Spring: the divers' season opens. Summer: clear water, crowded harbour. Autumn: storms move sand, uncovering wrecks. Winter: marine snow, cold deep water, ice on the piers |
| **Weather** | Rain: no drying, empty streets, fish come up. Fog: boats stay in; ghost stories. Storms: dangerous to swim; afterwards, new things washed ashore |
| **Story** | Places change as the town changes: a shop that turned her away puts out a water basin; the council hall gets protest banners |

## 3. Things to discover

### People and their stories
* **Wanderers:** people found in odd places with their own small story: a human boy fishing alone every dawn, an old merfolk diver who won't come ashore, a widow who leaves bread on the rocks.
* **Overheard moments:** two dockworkers arguing, a couple meeting secretly under the pier, children telling ghost stories about the Drowned Quarter.
* Following people often leads to new places.

### Lost things (return to their owners)
Things lost to the sea, found while exploring underwater. Each belongs to someone in town or at the Shelf.
* A wedding ring among the harbour pilings.
* A child's wooden boat in the sea caves.
* A drowned fisher's pocket watch, still engraved.
* A merfolk elder's carved comb lost in a storm decades ago.

Returning them gives a scene, a memory, sometimes a friendship or a barnacle. Some owners are gone; then she decides who should get it.

### Sea-glass letters
Messages in bottles and carved shells: letters from sailors, love notes, a child's wish, a merfolk song carved into shell. Collected in the journal. A few connect into small mysteries.

### Wrecks and ruins
* Shipwrecks with names, cargo and logs that tell what happened.
* The Drowned Quarter: streets and buildings of old Brinehaven under the sea.
* Merfolk ruins near Kelpcrown: carvings showing how merfolk once lived across all these islands.

### Creatures (the merfolk naming book)
* Fish, crabs, rays, turtles, seals, whales, octopuses and seabirds.
* Each creature has a **human name and a merfolk name**; learning both adds them to the journal, sketched in her hand.
* Some creatures are characters: an old turtle that circles the Pearl Banks; a curious seal that follows her around Gull Rock; a pod of whales passing the drop-off in autumn.

### Views
* Special spots to sit, rest or float: the lighthouse gallery, the top of the net-lofts, a hole in the reef where light falls straight down, the drop-off edge.
* Sitting here plays a short quiet moment: music, her thoughts, sometimes a friend joining.

### Sounds
* **Songs underwater:** merfolk songs carried on currents; following one leads somewhere.
* **The divers' whistles:** hearing an unfamiliar whistle means someone's out there; maybe in trouble.

### Shells and barnacles
Rare shells and pearls found exploring, used for festival lanterns and gifts (not a currency grind).

## 4. The map

* She buys a rough human chart in Act 1. It shows the islands, the harbour and a few shipping lanes, and nothing underwater.
* **She draws the rest herself.** Places she explores get sketched in. Underwater areas get her own notes in merfolk script.
* **The current map:** merfolk elders teach her the currents between islands by name. Each one learned appears as a flowing line and becomes a fast-travel route.
* **Two layers:** flip between the surface map and the underwater map.
* **Rumours:** things people mention show up as a faint question mark in the area they described, not an exact pin.
* No markers for every collectible.

## 5. How new places open

| Gate | Example |
|---|---|
| **Breath and depth** | Deep beds and the drop-off open as Breath grows through training dives with the elders |
| **Currents** | Learning a current's name from an elder makes it swimmable |
| **Words** | Kelpcrown elders won't let her in until she can greet them properly |
| **Friendships** | Keeper Holm lets her climb the lighthouse; Old Lis takes her to the salt caves; Corra shows her the hidden reef |
| **Tide and time** | The sea caves at low tide; bioluminescent reef only at night |
| **Seasons** | Spring opens the dive grounds; autumn storms uncover wrecks; winter cold blocks the deep without the elders' gift |
| **Her boat** | Distant islands and night trips with human friends |
| **Story** | The Drowned Quarter's sealed town hall opens in Act 3 |

## 6. Each island

| Place | Above | Below | Special to find |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brinehaven** | Harbour, market, rooftops, lighthouse hill, back alleys | Pilings forest under the piers, harbour floor full of lost things, drain tunnels | The secret meeting spot under the pier; the fisher's watch |
| **The Shelf** | Stilt houses, the Warming Ring, rock pools | Underwater homes, kelp gardens, kids' play caves | The oldest barnacle carvings; Wenna's first diving stone |
| **Saltpan Isle** | Salt pans, windmills, a dry, bright village | Shallow sea-grass meadows, turtles | The salt caves (with Old Lis); old songs |
| **Gull Rock** | The outer lighthouse, bird cliffs, wind | Seal colony, kelp forest, a sunken bell | Keeper Holm's son's boat; the curious seal |
| **Ferrow's Reach** | Proud fishing village, boat sheds, a chapel | Rocky reef, fish traps, cold water | Aunt Maren's hiding place; a superstition's real origin |
| **Kelpcrown Atoll** | A ring of rocks and a half-empty merfolk settlement | Ancient merfolk ruins, carvings, a lagoon | The history of merfolk on the islands |
| **The Pearl Banks** | Company boats and buoys | Oyster beds, the old turtle | Pearls; signs of the Company cutting corners |
| **The Drowned Quarter** | Only chimney tops at low tide | Sunken streets, the town hall, the clock tower | The founders' charter |
| **The Drop-off** | Open sea, passing ships; Company boats at night | Blue fading to black; whales; marine snow in winter | Where the way home begins |
| **The Far Reefs** | Open ocean beyond the drop-off | Untouched reefs, then raw dredge scars heading toward Coral Hollow | The dredging operation (Act 3) |

## 7. Night exploration

* **Bioluminescence:** the reef and the harbour glow when disturbed; she leaves a glowing trail.
* **Night creatures:** octopuses, glowing jellies, feeding rays.
* **The town at night:** lamplighters, tavern songs, couples on the sea wall, secret meetings.
* **Stars:** sit on a rock and learn the merfolk names for stars, used to navigate home.
* Some story beats only happen at night: a merfolk funeral at sea, a secret council meeting, the festival.

---

## 8. Danger without combat

| Hazard | What happens |
|---|---|
| Strong currents | Pull her off course; ride them or fight across |
| Depth and cold | Faster Breath drain; she must surface |
| Storms | Waves, poor visibility, debris; dangerous to swim far |
| Tangled nets | Snag her; she has to work free (or free a trapped creature) |
| Drying out on land | Sea-sheen fades; she slows down |
| Sharks | Rare, shy, never attack; but they make her (and the player) nervous |
| People | Being chased off a dock, turned away, or followed |

None of these end the game. The worst outcome is losing part of a haul or having to come back another time.

---

## 9. Rewards for exploring

* **Story:** people, lost things, letters and history that deepen the main plot.
* **Belonging:** returning lost things, helping wanderers and learning creatures and currents grow barnacles.
* **Words and customs** for the journal.
* **Festival materials:** rare shells and glowing pearls for the lanterns.
* **Places to rest** and quiet moments.
* **Cosmetics:** small items given by friends (a ribbon from Ada, a knitted wrap from Old Lis, a shell clasp from Wenna) she can wear, so her look tells her story.
